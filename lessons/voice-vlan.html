Voice VLAN

How a phone and a PC share one switch port

  • 2.1VLANs and inter-VLAN connectivity

The problem: two devices, one cable

Offices often have one network jack per desk. An IP phone (PHONE1) has a small built-in switch with two ports: one to the wall (here SW1 Fa0/1) and one for the PC (PC1). Both devices now share Fa0/1.

An access port normally carries one VLAN. Right now both the phone and the PC land in VLAN 10, the data VLAN. Voice traffic is mixed with data, cannot be given priority on its own, and the phone cannot use the voice network to reach its call server, CUCM.

Two VLANs on an access port

A voice VLAN lets one access port carry two VLANs:

  • The access VLAN (10) for the PC, sent untagged, as on any access port.
  • The voice VLAN (150) for the phone, sent with an 802.1Q tag that names VLAN 150.

It is still an access port, not a trunk. The PC's untagged frames go into VLAN 10, and the PC cannot reach other VLANs by tagging its own frames. The tag also has a 3-bit priority field (CoS), and phones normally mark voice CoS 5 so switches can queue it ahead of data.

How the phone learns its VLAN

  1. The switch advertises the voice VLAN to the phone in CDP (or LLDP-MED, for other vendors' phones).
  2. The phone starts tagging its own traffic with VLAN 150.
  3. It sends a new DHCP request, which now arrives in VLAN 150, at R1's subinterface G0/0.150. R1 answers from the VOICE pool with a 192.168.150.x address.
  4. The VOICE pool includes option 150, the address of the TFTP server the phone loads its configuration from; here that is the call server.
  5. Frames from the PC pass through the phone untagged and stay in VLAN 10.

Why voice gets its own VLAN

Voice is sensitive in a way data is not. A file download that pauses for a moment is fine; a call that loses or delays packets sounds broken. Keeping phones in their own VLAN and subnet makes it easy to:

  • recognise voice traffic and give it priority (QoS) all the way to the call server,
  • keep the PCs' broadcasts away from the phones,
  • apply separate security rules, so a PC cannot easily reach the call system.

Configured here

On SW1, the VLAN must exist first: vlan 150 and name VOICE. Then on Fa0/1, keep switchport access vlan 10 and add switchport voice vlan 150. R1 already routes both VLANs on subinterfaces G0/0.10 and G0/0.150 and has a DHCP pool for each. CDP shows the phone as SEP followed by its MAC address, with capability P (phone).

Check it worked

show interfaces fa0/1 switchport shows Access Mode VLAN 10 and Voice VLAN 150. show vlan brief lists Fa0/1 under both VLAN 10 and VLAN 150. After renewing, the phone has a 192.168.150.x address and pings the call server at 192.168.200.5, while the PC still has 192.168.10.x and reaches its gateway 192.168.10.1.

See it happen

In the app, these steps play out as packets moving across the lab's map.

  1. Before: everything is data With no voice VLAN on Fa0/1 the phone sends untagged frames, so they land in VLAN 10 and it gets a 192.168.10.x address like the PC.
  2. The switch tells the phone After switchport voice vlan 150, SW1 includes VLAN 150 in its CDP messages to the phone.
  3. The phone tags its traffic The phone tags its frames with VLAN 150 and CoS 5 and asks DHCP again. The request arrives on R1 G0/0.150 and is answered from the VOICE pool.
  4. The PC stays in data PC1's frames pass through the phone untagged. SW1 puts them in the access VLAN, 10, and the PC keeps its 192.168.10.x address.
  5. Voice reaches the call server From its 192.168.150.x address the phone reaches CUCM at 192.168.200.5, the server option 150 pointed it to.
  6. Replies come back tagged R1 sends the reply out G0/0.150, and SW1 delivers it to Fa0/1 tagged for VLAN 150, so it goes to the phone and not the PC.

Key terms

Voice VLAN
A second VLAN on an access port, used by an IP phone with 802.1Q tags.
Access VLAN
The VLAN for untagged frames on an access port, here the PC's data VLAN.
802.1Q tag
A 4-byte field added to a frame that carries the VLAN number and a priority.
CoS
Class of Service: the 3-bit priority field in the 802.1Q tag. Voice is usually 5.
CDP
Cisco Discovery Protocol, used here to tell the phone which voice VLAN to use.
DHCP option 150
Gives Cisco IP phones the address of the TFTP server they load their configuration from.

For the exam

  • 2.1: configure and verify VLANs (normal range) spanning multiple switches: access ports (data and voice), default VLAN, InterVLAN connectivity.
  • A port with a voice VLAN is still an access port: data untagged, voice tagged.
  • The command is switchport voice vlan on the interface; the VLAN should exist in the VLAN database.
  • show interfaces switchport shows the Voice VLAN line.
  • The switch tells a Cisco phone its voice VLAN through CDP.

Common mistakes

  • Making the port a trunk to carry two VLANs: it works, but the PC could then send tagged frames into any allowed VLAN. A voice VLAN is the intended way.
  • Forgetting to create VLAN 150: the port lists a voice VLAN that does not exist, so the phone's tagged traffic goes nowhere.
  • Disabling CDP on the port: a Cisco phone never learns the voice VLAN and stays in the data VLAN.
  • Not renewing the phone's address: it keeps its old 192.168.10.x lease until it asks again.

Quick check

1. A port has switchport access vlan 10 and switchport voice vlan 150. How are the PC's frames sent?

  1. Tagged with VLAN 10
  2. Untagged, in VLAN 10
  3. Tagged with VLAN 150
  4. Untagged, in VLAN 150
Show the answer

B. Untagged, in VLAN 10

The access VLAN is untagged as on any access port. Only the phone's voice traffic carries an 802.1Q tag.

2. How does a Cisco IP phone learn which voice VLAN to use?

  1. From DHCP option 150
  2. From the PC plugged into it
  3. From the switch, through CDP
  4. It always uses VLAN 1
Show the answer

C. From the switch, through CDP

The switch advertises the voice VLAN in CDP. Option 150 gives the TFTP server address, not the VLAN.

3. Which command confirms the access and voice VLANs on Fa0/1?

  1. show interfaces fa0/1 switchport
  2. show interfaces trunk
  3. show ip interface brief
  4. show mac address-table
Show the answer

A. show interfaces fa0/1 switchport

show interfaces switchport lists Access Mode VLAN and Voice VLAN. A voice port is not a trunk, so show interfaces trunk does not list it.

4. What priority value do IP phones usually mark in the 802.1Q tag for voice?

  1. CoS 0
  2. CoS 3
  3. CoS 7
  4. CoS 5
Show the answer

D. CoS 5

Voice is usually marked CoS 5 so switches can queue it ahead of data. 0 is best effort.
