802.1Q trunking

How one link carries many VLANs

  • 2.1VLANs and inter-VLAN connectivity
  • 2.2Trunks and 802.1Q

The problem trunks solve

A VLAN is a separate broadcast domain inside a switch. When VLAN 10 and VLAN 20 exist on both SW1 and SW2, the hosts in each VLAN need to reach their partners on the other switch. One cable per VLAN works, but it wastes ports and does not scale to dozens of VLANs.

A trunk is one link that carries frames for many VLANs. The catch: the switch at the far end must be able to tell which VLAN each frame belongs to.

The 802.1Q tag

IEEE 802.1Q solves it by inserting a 4-byte tag into the Ethernet frame, just after the source MAC address. Inside the tag is a 12-bit VLAN ID (usable values 1 to 4094) and a 3-bit priority field used by QoS.

  1. PC1 sends an ordinary untagged frame into SW1 Fa0/1, an access port in VLAN 10.
  2. SW1 sends it out the trunk G0/1 with a tag saying VLAN 10.
  3. SW2 reads the tag, removes it, and delivers the frame only to VLAN 10 ports.

The PCs never see a tag. ISL, an older Cisco-only method, is obsolete.

The native VLAN

One VLAN on a trunk travels untagged: the native VLAN, VLAN 1 by default. Any untagged frame that arrives on a trunk is placed in the native VLAN. Both ends must agree on it. If they do not, frames from one VLAN come out in another, and CDP reports a native VLAN mismatch. A common hardening step is to make the native VLAN an unused one.

VLANs on both ends, and the allowed list

A switch only forwards a VLAN it knows about, so VLAN 10 and 20 must be created on SW2 too. That is the lab's first step.

By default a trunk allows all VLANs, 1 to 4094. switchport trunk allowed vlan 10,20 restricts it to the VLANs in use, which keeps broadcasts of other VLANs off the link. To change the list later use switchport trunk allowed vlan add 30. Without add, the command replaces the whole list.

Forcing the trunk

switchport mode trunk makes the port a trunk unconditionally. Cisco switches can also negotiate with DTP (Dynamic Trunking Protocol): dynamic desirable actively asks to trunk, dynamic auto only agrees if asked. Two auto ends never form a trunk and stay access ports. Setting the mode by hand is clearer and safer, and switchport nonegotiate turns DTP off. Some switches that also support ISL need switchport trunk encapsulation dot1q before they accept trunk mode.

Check it worked

show interfaces trunk lists each trunk with its mode, encapsulation (802.1q), status (trunking) and native VLAN, then the allowed VLANs, the allowed VLANs that exist on the switch, and those actually forwarding. show interfaces g0/1 switchport shows the administrative and operational mode. Then ping PC3 from PC1 and PC4 from PC2.

See it happen

In the app, these steps play out as packets moving across the lab's map.

  1. Into an access port PC1 sends a frame for PC3. It arrives untagged on Fa0/1, and SW1 knows it is in VLAN 10 because of the port.
  2. Tagged on the trunk PC3 is reached through G0/1, a trunk. SW1 inserts an 802.1Q tag with VLAN ID 10 and sends the frame across.
  3. Delivered in VLAN 10 SW2 reads VLAN 10 from the tag, removes the tag, and sends the frame out Fa0/1, its VLAN 10 access port.
  4. Same cable, other VLAN PC2 is in VLAN 20, so its frames cross the same trunk tagged 20. The tag keeps the two VLANs apart on one wire.
  5. Delivered in VLAN 20 SW2 delivers the frame only to VLAN 20 ports. PC3 never sees it.
  6. Not on the allowed list If SW1 also had a VLAN 30, its frames would not be sent on G0/1, because the allowed list is only 10 and 20.

Key terms

Trunk
A link that carries frames for several VLANs, identifying each with a tag.
802.1Q
The IEEE standard for VLAN tagging on trunks.
VLAN tag
4 bytes added to a frame on a trunk, holding a 12-bit VLAN ID and a priority.
Native VLAN
The one VLAN sent untagged on an 802.1Q trunk. VLAN 1 by default.
Allowed VLAN list
The VLANs a trunk is permitted to carry. All by default.
DTP
Dynamic Trunking Protocol: Cisco's way for two ports to negotiate whether to trunk.

For the exam

  • 2.2: configure and verify interswitch connectivity: trunk ports, 802.1Q, native VLAN. 2.1: VLANs spanning multiple switches.
  • The 802.1Q tag is 4 bytes with a 12-bit VLAN ID. Native VLAN frames are untagged.
  • The native VLAN must match on both ends. The default is VLAN 1.
  • dynamic auto plus dynamic auto gives an access link; auto with desirable, or trunk with either, gives a trunk.
  • allowed vlan add appends; plain allowed vlan replaces the list.

Common mistakes

  • VLAN missing on one switch: the trunk is up but that VLAN does not pass. show interfaces trunk leaves it out of the allowed-and-active list.
  • Forgetting add when allowing a new VLAN: every other VLAN on the trunk suddenly stops working.
  • Native VLAN mismatch: CDP logs a mismatch and traffic in those VLANs leaks into each other.
  • Leaving both ends on dynamic auto: the link silently stays an access link in VLAN 1.

Quick check

1. How long is the VLAN ID field in an 802.1Q tag?

  1. 8 bits
  2. 12 bits
  3. 16 bits
  4. 32 bits
Show the answer

B. 12 bits

12 bits gives 4096 values, of which 1 to 4094 are usable. The whole tag is 4 bytes (32 bits), which is the tempting wrong answer.

2. How are frames in the native VLAN sent across an 802.1Q trunk?

  1. Tagged with VLAN 1
  2. Tagged with the native VLAN number
  3. Untagged
  4. They are not allowed on trunks
Show the answer

C. Untagged

The native VLAN is the one VLAN sent without a tag. A frame arriving untagged on a trunk is placed in the native VLAN.

3. Both ends of a link are set to switchport mode dynamic auto. What happens?

  1. The link becomes a trunk
  2. The link goes err-disabled
  3. The link becomes a trunk only for VLAN 1
  4. The link stays an access link
Show the answer

D. The link stays an access link

Auto waits to be asked. With two auto ends nobody asks, so no trunk forms.

4. A trunk carries VLANs 10 and 20. You enter switchport trunk allowed vlan 30. What happens?

  1. Only VLAN 30 is carried; 10 and 20 stop
  2. VLAN 30 is added to 10 and 20
  3. The command is rejected
  4. VLAN 30 becomes the native VLAN
Show the answer

A. Only VLAN 30 is carried; 10 and 20 stop

Without the add keyword the list is replaced. To append, use switchport trunk allowed vlan add 30.

5. The trunk is up and VLAN 10 pings work, but VLAN 20 pings fail. show vlan brief on SW2 has no VLAN 20. Why?

  1. The native VLAN is wrong
  2. VLAN 20 does not exist on SW2, so SW2 will not forward it
  3. The trunk needs a second cable for VLAN 20
  4. PC4 needs a tag
Show the answer

B. VLAN 20 does not exist on SW2, so SW2 will not forward it

A switch only forwards VLANs it has. Create VLAN 20 on SW2 and it appears in the trunk's active list.
